## Federal pressure rises

NHTSA is putting autonomous-vehicle developers on notice over how robotaxis behave around emergency scenes. TechCrunch reported that administrator Jonathan Morrison sent a directive to companies covered by the agency's Standing General Order for automated driving systems, saying failures to detect and appropriately respond to police, fire, and emergency activity should be treated as a functional insufficiency rather than an edge case.

The reported letter did not single out one company. But it landed after a series of visible robotaxi incidents in dense city environments, including San Francisco's July Fourth traffic disruption. KQED reported that Supervisor Bilal Mahmood planned a letter of inquiry asking city emergency, fire, and transit agencies how autonomous vehicles affected public transit, emergency response, and crowd egress that night.

## What changes

The practical question is no longer only whether a driverless car can complete trips in normal traffic. Regulators are pressing developers to prove that the software can recognize unusual but expected urban conditions, coordinate with public-safety workers, and avoid becoming an obstacle when roads are congested or controlled by first responders.

TechCrunch reported that NHTSA asked AV developers to present solutions by the end of July. That gives the robotaxi industry a short window to show operational fixes before federal pressure hardens into a more formal enforcement or rulemaking path.
