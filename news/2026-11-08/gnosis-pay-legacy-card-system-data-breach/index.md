Gnosis Pay disclosed on November 6 that unauthorized access to its legacy consumer card system exposed customer information. The company said its monitoring systems detected the incident on November 3 and that containment began within two hours.

According to the disclosure, the breach was limited to the system powering **Gnosis Pay-branded cards**. Its enterprise platform, which powers white-label card programs, was not affected. Gnosis Pay also said customer funds were unaffected.

The company said it replaced affected security credentials and addressed the vulnerability. It has increased monitoring of card activity and wallets, and made **3D Secure verification mandatory for all online card payments**. Those measures will remain in place until the legacy system's planned retirement on December 20, 2026.

Gnosis Pay said it emailed every affected customer with the specific information taken from their account, notified partners, and was communicating with relevant data-protection authorities and its card issuer. The public notice does not give an affected-customer count or a complete list of exposed data fields.

The retirement predates this disclosure: a September 3 company update outlined a transition away from direct-to-consumer cards and the web app toward partner-operated services on its enterprise platform.

For customers, the immediate concern is misuse of personal information, even where funds remain intact. Gnosis Pay warned that fraudulent messages may include genuine personal details or purchases. It advised customers to open official apps directly and never share seed phrases, private keys, PINs or full card numbers, or move funds in response to unsolicited messages.
