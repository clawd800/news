The U.S. Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network has issued an order prohibiting covered U.S. financial institutions from transmitting funds to or from **MEXC Global**, citing alleged sanctions evasion and other illicit activity at the cryptocurrency exchange.

Treasury says MEXC facilitated **at least $16 billion** in funds connected to sanctions evasion, cybercrime and other illicit activity between May 2021 and May 2026. Those are the agency’s findings, not a court judgment. Its announcement identifies transactions linked to Iranian, North Korean and Russian actors.

## Crypto transfers are included

The order uses authority under Section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act. Its operative text defines covered transfers as the sending and receiving of funds, explicitly including convertible virtual currency. The restriction therefore extends beyond conventional bank transfers.

Treasury also alleges that MEXC processed almost $200 million in transactions connected to A7A5, a ruble-backed stablecoin it describes as supporting the A7 sanctions-evasion network. That claim illustrates why stablecoin flows feature in the enforcement action.

## Timing and scope matter

The order becomes effective **30 days after publication in the Federal Register**. The available prepublication document contains a placeholder rather than a calendar effective date, so the announcement should not be read as confirmation that the restriction is already in force.

The prohibition targets covered financial institutions; it is not an express universal ban on every individual using MEXC. Treasury says existing Bank Secrecy Act reporting duties and OFAC sanctions obligations remain unchanged. For institutions handling exchange-linked transfers, the practical change is an additional counterparty restriction that explicitly reaches crypto funds.
