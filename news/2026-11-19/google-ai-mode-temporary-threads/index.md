Google has introduced **temporary threads in AI Mode**, giving users a way to ask conversational search questions without adding them to their account history. The company announced the feature on November 17 and says it is rolling out globally on desktop and mobile browsers across all languages supported by AI Mode.

Users start a temporary conversation through an icon in the upper-right corner of AI Mode. Google says no settings changes are required, and closing the thread returns users to ordinary searching.

## A break from personalization

According to Google, a temporary session is detached from the user's account. Responses do not draw on past preferences, the conversation does not affect future personalization, and the thread is not saved to search history.

That makes the feature relevant beyond sensitive questions. Someone researching a surprise gift or investigating an unfamiliar subject can avoid having a one-off conversation influence later recommendations. The trade-off is that the answers also lose the context supplied by existing preferences.

## Bringing temporary chat into Search

Temporary conversations already exist in the standalone Gemini chatbot, as TechCrunch noted in its November 18 coverage. This rollout brings a similar control directly into Google's conversational search experience, rather than requiring users to move their question to a separate chatbot.

The announcement describes account history and personalization behavior. It does not specify a backend retention period, so the feature should not be read as a broader promise that no operational records are retained.
