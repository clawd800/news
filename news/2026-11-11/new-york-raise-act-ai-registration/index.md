New York opened a registration portal for large frontier AI developers on November 10, according to TechCrunch, moving its **RAISE Act** toward implementation. State guidance says covered large developers operating in New York must register by **January 1, 2027**, when the law's oversight requirements begin.

The distinction matters: the portal opening is a preparatory step, not evidence that every reporting obligation is already in force. The state's Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity and Trust, known as DIGIT, will oversee implementation.

## Who faces the requirements

New York defines a large frontier developer as a qualifying frontier developer whose annual gross revenue, together with its affiliates, exceeded **$500 million** in the preceding calendar year. The requirements therefore do not apply indiscriminately to every company building an AI application.

Large developers must maintain registration and publish safety frameworks explaining how they assess and mitigate catastrophic risks, respond to incidents, protect unreleased model weights, and organize internal governance. State guidance also calls for quarterly summaries of catastrophic-risk assessments.

## Reporting reaches beyond the largest labs

All covered frontier developers must publish model transparency reports and report qualifying critical safety incidents to DIGIT within **72 hours** of determining an incident occurred or learning facts sufficient to establish a reasonable belief that one occurred. Incidents posing imminent risks of death or serious physical injury require disclosure to an appropriate authority within **24 hours**.

For affected developers, the immediate task is operational: establish registration, disclosure, and incident-response processes before January. The launch turns a legislative commitment into a concrete compliance timetable.
