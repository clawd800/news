Google announced a preview of its **Connected Apps Platform** on October 28, giving developers a shared workflow for bringing third-party services into Gemini and Search. The change extends the existing consumer integrations with a platform for registering, testing, and submitting additional apps.

## One integration, two destinations

Developers connect a cloud-hosted Model Context Protocol server or use Android MCP through the AppFunctions APIs. They then use the Google Connected Console to register and test the integration before submitting it for review.

Before review, developers choose whether their app should appear in Search, the Gemini app, or both. Once an integration is live, the console provides traffic, latency, and error-rate monitoring. Google also says developers retain control over brand identity, including verified logos and authenticated accounts.

Access is not yet universal. Google says it is admitting developers in phases and plans to add markets and Google products over the coming months. TechCrunch reports that the initial preview is available to U.S. developers through a waitlist.

## Distribution beyond the standalone app

Google says integrations from Adobe, Experian, Localiza, and Peloton are already live in Gemini and will reach Search in the coming weeks. A Calm integration is also planned for both products.

For developers, the concrete change is a common integration and submission path into two AI-facing distribution surfaces. That could reduce duplicate integration work, but a submission is not an immediate launch: review and phased access still determine availability. The announcement does not establish how much additional traffic participating apps will receive.
