Netflix disclosed in its latest quarterly filing that it completed a March acquisition for approximately $587 million in cash, adding a concrete price tag to one of its quieter artificial intelligence moves this year.

The filing describes the transaction as a business combination but does not name the acquired company. TechCrunch and Variety both identify the target as InterPositive, an AI filmmaking startup co-founded by Ben Affleck. The reports say the deal was completed in March, matching the timing in Netflix's Form 10-Q.

The disclosure matters because it gives investors and production teams a clearer view of how much Netflix is willing to spend on AI-native production infrastructure. The company has already said generative AI is being used across hundreds of titles, mostly in post-production workflows, but acquisitions point to a deeper effort to own more of the underlying tooling.

Netflix has not framed the transaction as a replacement for conventional production. The more immediate signal is that the company sees AI-assisted media tools as strategic enough to justify a large cash purchase, while still keeping details about the acquired operation limited in public filings.

For studios, creators, and vendors, the deal adds another marker that AI production tools are moving from experiments into the balance sheets of major entertainment companies. The open question is how Netflix integrates InterPositive's technology into actual projects, and how much of that work becomes visible to audiences or creators.
