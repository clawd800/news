Meta has removed an Instagram Stories AI feature that let users modify photos from public accounts, reversing course after complaints that the tool crossed a line around how public posts could be reused.

The feature was part of a broader update to AI effects in Instagram Stories. According to TechCrunch, it allowed people to use Meta AI to alter images from public Instagram accounts. Meta later said the tool was no longer available, writing that its intent had been to provide a creative feature while giving people control over whether their public content could be referenced.

The reversal matters because it shows how quickly social platforms can run into user resistance when generative AI touches identity and personal media, even when the source material is technically public. Public sharing on Instagram has long implied distribution inside the app, but remixing another person's photo with AI introduces a different expectation: the post becomes raw material for a new synthetic image.

Meta's statement framed the removal as a response to feedback rather than a broader retreat from AI editing. The company continues to ship AI effects and creation tools across its apps. For Instagram, the episode adds a practical boundary for future launches: controls and consent language may need to be clear before AI features reuse public content in ways users can immediately recognize.
