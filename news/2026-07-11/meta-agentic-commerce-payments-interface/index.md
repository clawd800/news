Meta chief data officer Alex Schultz is describing **agentic commerce** as a possible next layer of Meta's business, but his framing is cautious about what the company would actually own.

In a CoinDesk Spotlight interview, Schultz said Meta sees a future where digital payments and conversational interfaces become more central to commerce. He called agentic commerce "the next tier of business" for the company, while pointing to systems such as WeChat and Line as examples of chat-based commerce infrastructure that reached mainstream use in Asia.

The important distinction is that Schultz did not present Meta as trying to revive a proprietary currency strategy. According to CoinDesk, he described Meta's likely role as an interface layer: messaging, social surfaces, and commerce interactions on top of payment systems operated with partners.

That matters for the stablecoin and AI-agent market because many agentic-commerce proposals assume software will need ways to hold value, pay merchants, and settle transactions without a human clicking through every step. Schultz's comments suggest Meta is watching the same direction, but still sees adoption, reliability, and scale as open problems.

The conservative read is that this is positioning, not a launch. Meta is not announcing a payments product in the interview, and Schultz said no current system has yet reached the scale and reliability required for Meta to go all in.

Still, the comments are notable because they place agentic commerce inside Meta's strategic vocabulary, alongside messaging, digital payments, and partner-led settlement.
