CME Group's challenge to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission has turned crypto-style perpetual futures into a live court test for U.S. market structure.

## What changed

On May 29, the CFTC approved KalshiEX's BTCPERP contract, a perpetual contract tied to the spot price of bitcoin, for listing as a futures contract on a designated contract market. The agency also issued a policy statement saying perpetual contracts should generally be reviewed case by case under Commission Regulation 40.3, especially when they reference asset classes beyond the bitcoin product covered by the order.

CME then sued the CFTC and Chairman Mike Selig, challenging the agency's decision to let Kalshi and Coinbase list crypto perpetuals. CoinDesk reports that CME argues the products are being misclassified and that perpetuals, which have no expiration date and use ongoing funding mechanics, should not be treated the same way as traditional futures.

## Why it matters

The fight is not just about one bitcoin contract. Perpetuals dominate offshore crypto derivatives trading, and onchain venues have made 24/7 leveraged markets familiar to crypto-native users. Bringing similar products onto regulated U.S. platforms could reshape how exchanges, prediction markets, and DeFi-adjacent venues compete.

The CFTC's order was narrow, but its policy statement opened a process for more applications. CME's lawsuit asks the court to slow or reverse that path. Until the case is resolved, U.S. perpetuals remain a regulatory experiment rather than settled infrastructure.
