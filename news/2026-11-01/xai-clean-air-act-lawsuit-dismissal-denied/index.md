A federal court has rejected **xAI's motion to dismiss an NAACP Clean Air Act lawsuit**, allowing the challenge to the company's data-center power infrastructure to proceed. The Northern District of Mississippi issued the ruling on October 30, according to Earthjustice, which represents the NAACP alongside the Southern Environmental Law Center.

The case concerns a gas-turbine power plant in **Southaven, Mississippi**, supporting xAI's Colossus 2 data center. The NAACP alleges the company operated the plant without required air permits. The ruling keeps those claims alive; it is not a final judgment resolving the entire lawsuit.

## Two defenses rejected

xAI argued that the turbines qualified for a mobile-source exemption from permitting requirements. The court rejected that position, finding that the plant was not a mobile source under the applicable rules and therefore was not exempt from Clean Air Act permitting.

The company also challenged the constitutionality of citizen suits. Judge Debra M. Brown concluded that plaintiffs suing in their own names to redress their own injuries were not exercising the federal government's executive power. TechCrunch independently reported the dismissal decision and the court's rejection of these defenses.

## Injunction request remains unresolved

Earthjustice said the court had not yet ruled on the NAACP's request for a preliminary injunction. The dismissal ruling therefore should not be read as an order shutting down the plant.

For AI infrastructure operators, the dispute highlights a concrete constraint on rapid capacity expansion: power-generation equipment serving a data center remains subject to environmental permitting requirements, even when described as temporary.
