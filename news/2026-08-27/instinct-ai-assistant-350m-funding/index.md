Instinct, a private-beta personal AI assistant from Spear Street Technology, has reportedly raised a new **$250 million Series B**, bringing total funding to **$350 million** and valuing the company at **$2.5 billion**. TechCrunch reported the financing based on a Wall Street Journal interview with the company, saying the round was co-led by Index Ventures and Benchmark.

The company describes Instinct as a personal assistant that understands what a user is working on and can act across applications and devices. Founder Noah Shinn has framed the product as a personal agent that users can text or call, with early testers using it for tasks such as trip planning, groceries, concert tickets, subscription cancellations, and wedding planning.

That ambition is also the risk. A general-purpose personal agent becomes useful only when it has enough access to observe context and take action. TechCrunch separately reported concerns from early users and observers about Instinct's permissions, terms, and ability to act on a user's behalf.

The funding therefore says something larger than another hot AI valuation. Investors are still willing to back consumer agents that promise to operate across everyday software, even before the category has settled basic expectations around consent, auditability, revocation, and data boundaries.

The conservative takeaway is that Instinct is becoming an important test case for personal agents. Its traction suggests demand for assistants that do more than chat, while the privacy debate shows why agent access may become the real product surface.
