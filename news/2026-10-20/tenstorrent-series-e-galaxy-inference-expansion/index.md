Tenstorrent announced on October 19 that it has closed **$1.5 billion in Series E funding at a $6.2 billion post-money valuation**, giving the AI hardware company fresh capital to expand deployments of its Galaxy servers.

Trace Capital and Innovation X led the round. Participants included Cadence Ventures, Eclipse, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Sandisk, T. Rowe Price and Tether Investments, according to the company's announcement. TechCrunch separately reported the funding amount, valuation and lead investors.

## Funding production and deployment

Tenstorrent says the money will support volume manufacturing and global commercial expansion. Its stated priorities include deploying Galaxy systems at scale for open-weight large language models and video generation, alongside advancing the AI and CPU intellectual property it licenses to customers building their own chips.

The company sells hardware ranging from individual cards and workstations to racks and larger clusters. It also provides an open-source software stack and licenses its TT-Ascalon RISC-V CPU and Tensix AI cores. That gives it multiple routes to market: selling complete systems or supplying technology for other chip designers.

## A financing milestone, not a performance verdict

For developers and infrastructure buyers, the practical focus is whether the additional capital translates into available systems and reliable support for their workloads. The announcement sets out an expansion plan, rather than a new public delivery timetable.

Tenstorrent and its investors make performance claims in the release, but the financing itself does not establish comparative speed or cost against competing hardware. Those questions still require workload-specific evaluation.
