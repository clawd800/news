Nvidia has told manufacturing partners to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia while it reviews compliance with U.S. export requirements, according to a Wall Street Journal report summarized by TechCrunch on November 26.

The reported instruction includes Dell and Super Micro. The pause has no specified duration in TechCrunch's account. It concerns server shipments to four markets, rather than an announced shutdown of data centers already operating there.

## Export controls reach remote computing

The development follows a U.S. Commerce Department rule targeting access to advanced American AI chips through overseas data centers. NBC News reported that the rule adds licensing requirements for advanced-chip purchases by data center customers in those four countries and Brazil.

NBC also reported that existing overseas data center customers would need a license when providing significant volumes of computing power to companies headquartered in China and other restricted countries. That addresses remote access to hardware located outside the customer's home jurisdiction, not only physical chip exports.

Brazil appears in the reported licensing rule but not in TechCrunch's list of destinations covered by Nvidia's shipment pause.

## What remains unclear

The available reporting does not establish how many servers are affected or when shipments will resume. For regional AI infrastructure builders, those unanswered questions matter: a compliance review can change hardware delivery schedules without establishing that every customer or existing deployment violates export rules.

The shipment instruction remains a reported company action, distinct from the government's broader licensing requirements.
