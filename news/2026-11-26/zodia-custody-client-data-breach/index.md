Zodia Custody has disclosed a security incident involving **unauthorized access to limited client information**. The institutional crypto custodian said its investigation had found no unauthorized transactions to date, distinguishing the reported data exposure from a confirmed theft of digital assets.

In a public statement, CEO Richard Clark said Zodia detected attempts to probe its platform on November 23. The company temporarily took its business-to-business API offline as a precaution, added security measures and then restored the service.

Monitoring on November 24 identified that an unauthorized party had accessed information, according to Clark. Zodia described the exposed material as publicly available organization details and, for a small number of clients and employees, a business contact name and email address.

## What is confirmed

The company said it had contacted clients and informed the relevant regulatory authorities. Decrypt separately reported the same incident timeline and the company's finding that no unauthorized transactions had been identified.

Zodia's statement does not give an exact count of affected people or organizations, identify the unauthorized party, or explain the technical route used to access the information. Its transaction finding is explicitly limited to the investigation so far.

For institutional custody customers, the disclosure separates two security questions: whether assets were moved without authorization and whether business contact information was exposed. Zodia reports evidence of the latter, while saying it has not found the former. Further technical findings would be needed to establish the incident's full scope.
