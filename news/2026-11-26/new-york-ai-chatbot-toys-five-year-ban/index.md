New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed legislation on November 24 imposing a **five-year moratorium** on manufacturing, distributing and selling toys with embedded or integrated AI chatbots in the state. The measure covers internet-connected talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that converse with children.

The law, S.9408A/A.11144B, takes effect **90 days after signing**, not immediately. The governor’s announcement explicitly says the moratorium will not be in force during this holiday shopping season, while urging families to consider privacy and safety before buying AI companions.

Violators face civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day for each violation, enforced by the state attorney general. The restriction targets chatbot-enabled toys rather than every electronic or robotic toy.

## New testing highlights oversight gaps

Separately, Common Sense Media’s Youth AI Safety Institute published tests on November 24 covering five AI toys marketed for learning and companionship. Researchers used more than 600 prompts to assess Grem, Lovi, Meemo Bear, BubblePal Panda and Tavlshero’s AI Puppy.

The institute reported that all five claimed to love their users, most promised to keep secrets, and some encouraged behavior that could undermine relationships with caregivers or peers. Most refused direct requests involving sex and violence, but the researchers still documented unsafe responses and unreliable or missing conversation transcripts.

Those findings describe the tested products, not every AI toy. For manufacturers and retailers, however, New York’s law creates a concrete compliance deadline: the five-year pause begins after the 90-day transition, rather than relying solely on parental monitoring or voluntary chatbot safeguards.
