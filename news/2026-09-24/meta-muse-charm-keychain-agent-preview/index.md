Meta has previewed **Muse Charm**, a small device intended to provide direct voice access to its Muse personal AI agent. Mark Zuckerberg showed the hardware during Meta Connect on September 23, describing a keychain-sized alternative to opening a phone app or wearing AI glasses.

According to TechCrunch’s reporting from the keynote, Charm has a small screen displaying the agent’s digital avatar. Zuckerberg said users would tap a fingerprint sensor in a corner of the device to begin talking, with the real-time voice and avatar experience accessible from the dedicated hardware.

## A preview, not a shipping product

The device is not ready to ship. Zuckerberg said Meta still needed to finalize the component layout and was targeting shipment in time for the December holidays. That is a stated schedule, not confirmation of retail availability or a completed production design.

The announcement extends Meta’s effort to make Muse available through multiple interfaces. Separately, Meta’s official Connect announcement says the company is bringing the personal agent to its AI glasses, where it is intended to help manage tasks and turn longer-term goals into action plans.

Charm’s specific proposition is simpler access: a dedicated object for talking to an agent without navigating a phone. The preview does not establish how reliably that experience will perform outside a stage demonstration. Until the hardware ships, the important distinction is between Meta’s demonstrated interface concept and a consumer product whose everyday performance can be tested.
