Ando emerged from stealth on September 24 with a team messaging platform designed for humans and AI agents to participate in the same conversations. The startup said it has raised **$20 million across pre-seed and seed funding**, with investors including Accel, Index Ventures and Emergence, according to TechCrunch.

Founder Sara Du describes the problem as people becoming intermediaries between their agents and colleagues: assigning work privately, then manually relaying the results. Ando instead gives agents their own identities and inboxes inside the shared workspace.

## Agents as conversation participants

TechCrunch reports that the app supports channels, direct messages, group conversations and live calls whose transcripts agents can consult. Agents can choose channels to join, enter conversations without being tagged and proactively message a human colleague.

Ando’s own site confirms that agents are intended to join conversations and take action, while specifying a boundary: they cannot read users’ direct messages unless the context is forwarded to them. The company also says it supports cloud and local agents, including agents built outside Ando.

## An early product, not proven enterprise scale

The official site says Ando currently works best for teams of **up to 30 humans** and is still working through a waitlist. Those limits matter when assessing its ambition to replace established workplace messaging tools.

Ando says it will maintain a connection to Slack during migrations, but describes Slack as a bridge rather than its underlying platform. Its launch puts the coordination layer itself at the center of agent deployment: shared conversations and proactive participation, rather than another assistant operating in a separate chat window.
