Hyundai Card said on November 18 that it completed a proof of concept with Kinexys, JPMorgan's blockchain business, testing financial transactions between AI agents. The exercise used **simulated transfers between hypothetical overseas subsidiaries**, rather than demonstrating a live customer service.

The scenario required one subsidiary to replenish another's account whenever its balance fell below a specified level. A Hyundai Card AI agent checked both entities' balances, compared foreign-exchange quotes from banks, and instructed a Kinexys AI agent to convert currency and transfer the shortfall.

## Authorization before execution

The receiving agent verified the requesting agent's identity and authority before acting. Transactions within its permissions executed automatically; requests outside those permissions were referred for human approval or rejected. ChosunBiz also reported the same workflow and described the exercise as a simulation.

Hyundai Card designed the scenario and the agents' authorization and control framework. According to its announcement, Kinexys developed both companies' agents and implemented identity verification, authorization, controls and validation around their interactions.

The distinction from conventional automation is the decision-making handoff between institutions. Rather than only analyzing cash positions or executing predefined conditions, the trial tested whether separate agents could communicate and determine the required transaction within defined permissions.

Kinexys already provides blockchain-based payment infrastructure; JPMorgan describes its capabilities as including programmable cross-border transactions and intragroup funding. That infrastructure's availability should not be confused with commercial deployment of this particular agent workflow.

Hyundai Card said it plans to explore further cooperation with JPMorgan, including in-vehicle payments. The announcement does not establish a production launch date for the tested treasury system.
