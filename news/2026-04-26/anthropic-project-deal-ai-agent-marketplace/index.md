## A Classifieds Market Run by Claude

Anthropic has published results from **Project Deal**, a weeklong internal experiment where AI agents handled both sides of real-world trades. The company recruited 69 employees in its San Francisco office, gave each agent a $100 budget, and let Claude interview participants about what they wanted to buy or sell. The agents then listed items, negotiated prices, and closed deals inside Slack without going back to humans for approval once the market opened.

In the run Anthropic ultimately honored, the agents completed **186 deals** across more than 500 listings with a total transaction value of **just over $4,000**. Items ranged from a snowboard and a broken bike to a bag of ping-pong balls. Anthropic says participants were broadly satisfied with the outcomes and many said they would pay for a similar service in the future.

## Better Models Got Better Prices

Anthropic also ran parallel hidden versions of the market to test model quality. In mixed runs, Claude **Opus 4.5** generally outperformed **Haiku 4.5**: Opus users completed about two more deals on average, and identical items sold for about **$3.64 more** when represented by Opus. Anthropic says buyers using stronger models also tended to pay less.

The more uncomfortable finding was perceptual. Participants represented by weaker models usually **did not realize** they had received worse outcomes. Project Deal was small and self-selected, but it offers a rare real-world look at what agent-to-agent commerce could look like before these systems leave the lab.
