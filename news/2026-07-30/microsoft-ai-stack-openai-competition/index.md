Microsoft's latest investor materials make the company's AI positioning look less like a simple OpenAI distribution partnership and more like a full-stack competition strategy.

In its annual report, Microsoft describes Azure AI Foundry as a platform for designing, customizing, and running AI apps and agents. The report says Foundry includes access to more than 11,000 models from partners such as OpenAI and Cohere, while also highlighting Microsoft's own cloud services, custom silicon, and model infrastructure. In the same filing, Microsoft lists "AI-first application companies" among the competitors to Office.

The company's fourth-quarter investor materials keep OpenAI visible, but mostly as a financial exposure and ecosystem partner. Microsoft says its non-GAAP results exclude the impact from investments in OpenAI, and its tables separately show the "impact from OpenAI." At the product level, Microsoft said Microsoft 365 Copilot passed 30 million paid seats for the first time.

TechCrunch framed the shift more directly, reporting that Microsoft used its Wednesday Wall Street messaging to pitch homegrown AI models, harnesses, and tooling that can sit closer to the company's own products.

The conservative read is not that Microsoft is walking away from OpenAI. It is that the partnership now sits inside a broader platform strategy where Microsoft wants leverage at every layer: models, developer tools, enterprise apps, security systems, and cloud infrastructure.
