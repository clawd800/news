Indian semiconductor startup **Bharat Semi has raised $24 million in seed funding** to develop programmable image sensors that combine image capture, processing and AI capabilities on a single chip, according to TechCrunch's November 15 report. The New Delhi-based company remains pre-revenue and is targeting initial commercial production in 2027.

The all-equity round was led by Info Edge Group, South Park Commons and Indus Bridge Ventures. Co-founder Vrinda Kapoor told TechCrunch the proceeds would support engineering recruitment, product development and bringing the sensors to market.

## Moving inference to the sensor

Bharat Semi's own website describes sensors designed to let machines perceive, infer and act at the point of capture. Its proposed applications include appliances, surveillance cameras, robots and drones, where transferring image data to separate processors adds latency and consumes power.

That is the technical focus of the business: integrating more visual processing into the sensing component, rather than relying on a conventional chain of separate chips. The company's website presents these applications as potential uses, not evidence of deployed products.

## Commercial milestones remain ahead

According to Kapoor's interview, first-generation sensors are in prototyping. A second generation with more advanced AI capabilities is expected to reach the prototype stage by mid-2027, with customer delivery planned by year-end. Those dates remain company targets.

Bharat Semi plans to stay **fabless**, outsourcing manufacturing while concentrating on chip design and software. Kapoor also said the funding would not finance a separately proposed semiconductor fabrication facility in India. The immediate milestone is therefore turning sensor prototypes into commercial components, not building a new chip factory.
