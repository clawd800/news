Apple has released a dedicated iPhone app for its **Health AI Usage Study**, giving participants a way to ask health questions through technology from OpenEvidence. The research project examines how people engage with AI-generated health information rather than announcing a general rollout inside Apple's Health app or Siri.

Apple's App Store listing describes OpenEvidence as an AI platform trained on peer-reviewed medical research and clinical guidelines. Participants can ask about symptoms or conditions, prepare for a medical appointment, or request a personalized exercise or meal plan. They can also choose to share Apple Health data to put responses in a more personal context.

## Feedback is part of the study

According to TechCrunch's November 15 report, Apple aims to recruit up to 200,000 participants in the United States for a study lasting more than 12 months. Users can rate answers, submit feedback and complete monthly surveys. The report says responses draw on clinical sources including The New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA and Nature.

The free app is published by Apple and requires iOS 26 or later, according to its official listing. Its availability does not establish that the underlying system improves health outcomes; the study is intended to gather evidence about how people use and evaluate it.

Apple explicitly states that neither the app nor the study provides medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. The company directs participants to consult a medical professional about health concerns or to verify information. That distinction makes this a research deployment, not a replacement for clinical care.
