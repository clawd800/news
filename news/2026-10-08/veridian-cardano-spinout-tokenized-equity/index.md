The Cardano Foundation has spun out **Veridian** as an independent digital-identity company, pairing the separation with tokenized equity on Cardano. CoinDesk and crypto.news reported the announcement on October 8; Veridian’s own website confirms its independent status.

The Swiss company is led by Thomas A. Mayfield and plans to seek strategic investors in 2027, according to the reports. Its identity infrastructure serves governments, businesses and software agents that need to prove who they are and what they are authorized to do.

## Equity with transfer rules

Veridian’s shares use **CIP-0113**, Cardano’s programmable-token framework. The specification defines tokens that require successful script execution before ownership changes. It supports controls such as approved-address lists and identity requirements, with regulated stablecoins and tokenized securities among the intended uses.

The framework uses existing Cardano capabilities rather than requiring a hard fork. These are controls for assets created under the framework, not a blanket change to ADA or ordinary native tokens.

According to crypto.news, the Foundation described Veridian’s shares as ledger-based securities under Switzerland’s DLT Act. The issuance provides a concrete corporate-equity application for the token standard.

## Credentials for software agents

Veridian says its infrastructure uses the KERI and ACDC protocols. Both reports identify Masumi, a Cardano-based agent payment and identity network, as an existing user. The intended workflow lets counterparties verify an agent’s credentials before payment and revoke credentials if the agent is compromised.

That connects two distinct infrastructure needs: enforcing rules when assets move and verifying the authority of the software initiating transactions. The spinout establishes a commercial vehicle for that work; it does not itself demonstrate broad adoption.
