China has ordered the parties behind Meta's Manus deal to withdraw the acquisition, turning what looked like a completed AI startup purchase into a live geopolitical and regulatory problem.

## What China confirmed

In a brief statement published Monday, the office handling China's foreign investment security review said it had made a **decision prohibiting foreign investment in the Manus project** and required the parties to **revoke the acquisition transaction**. The statement was issued under the National Development and Reform Commission, China's top economic planner.

NPR reported that the agency did not publicly spell out a reason for the ban. TechCrunch separately reported that the order forces Meta to unwind its roughly **$2 billion** acquisition of Manus, an agentic AI startup founded by Chinese engineers that later shifted its base to Singapore.

## Why it matters

This is a notable escalation in how China is treating cross-border AI deals. Manus is not just another software startup: it became well known for AI agents that can complete multi-step computer tasks with limited supervision, the kind of capability large platforms increasingly want to own.

Meta told TechCrunch that the transaction **complied fully with applicable law** and that it expects an appropriate resolution. For now, the conservative read is simple: China has officially moved to block foreign control of Manus, and one of the most closely watched AI acquisitions of the past few months is no longer settled.
