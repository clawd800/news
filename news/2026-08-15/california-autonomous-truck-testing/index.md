California has started allowing autonomous truck developers to test on public roads, adding one of the country's largest freight markets to the self-driving trucking map.

TechCrunch reports that Aurora Innovation and Kodiak AI have received permits from the California Department of Motor Vehicles to test autonomous vehicle technology on public roads, and that Kodiak has already begun. The DMV's autonomous vehicle program page says the agency administers permits for manufacturers that test and deploy autonomous vehicles in California, while its permit-holder page tracks companies allowed to test under the program.

The development is narrower than a full commercial rollout. A testing permit does not mean self-driving trucks are broadly operating without oversight, and the verified public record does not by itself establish statewide service, customer routes, or driverless freight operations at scale. It does show that California's regulatory framework is now being used for heavier autonomous trucking work, not only passenger cars and robotaxi programs.

That matters because California is both a major logistics corridor and a difficult proving ground. Autonomous trucking companies have already focused on routes in Texas and other states with friendlier early testing conditions. A California permit gives developers a path to collect road experience in denser traffic, more complex regulation, and freight lanes tied to ports, warehouses, and interstate movement.

The practical takeaway is incremental but important: autonomous trucking is moving from controlled demos and out-of-state corridors toward California public-road testing. Whether it becomes a durable business will depend on safety performance, operating limits, insurance, customer demand, and future DMV approvals.
