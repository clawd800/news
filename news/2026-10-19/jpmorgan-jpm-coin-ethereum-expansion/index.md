JPMorgan plans to bring **JPM Coin**, its token representing dollar deposits at the bank, to Ethereum, CoinDesk reported on October 19, citing a Bloomberg interview. The expansion would take the institutional payment instrument beyond its existing deployment on Base, Coinbase's Ethereum layer-2 network.

Emma Landriault, head of Kinexys Labs, told Bloomberg that Ethereum's liquidity and existing institutional usage made it attractive, according to CoinDesk. Potential users include crypto businesses making blockchain payments and financial firms settling trades or backing tokenized assets.

**No Ethereum launch date was disclosed.** CoinDesk said JPMorgan had not responded to its request for additional details by publication. The bank's public product documentation still identifies Base as the network where JPM Coin is issued, so the report should not be read as confirmation of a live Ethereum service.

## A deposit token, not an open-access stablecoin

JPMorgan describes JPM Coin as a bank-issued deposit token backed by U.S. dollar deposits held in its accounts, rather than a stablecoin. Although transactions use a public blockchain, participation is restricted to vetted, approved counterparties.

The product operates through the bank's Blockchain Deposit Account framework, connecting existing treasury infrastructure with on-chain activity. Documented uses include cross-border payments, intraday liquidity transfers, collateral posting and programmable settlement.

For Ethereum, the significance is a potential additional institutional cash instrument on the network. Public-chain deployment does not, however, imply permissionless access: JPMorgan's existing compliance and onboarding requirements remain central to the product. The next concrete milestone is an announced availability date and implementation details for the Ethereum deployment.
