Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr warned on November 16 that **AI agents could accelerate bank runs by moving customer deposits autonomously**, potentially compressing outflows into minutes. His remarks at an artificial-intelligence-in-finance conference in Milan described a potential risk, not an observed AI-triggered bank run.

Barr said agents could detect differences in yields or signals about a bank’s stability and transfer funds at high speed. During stress, that automation could overwhelm the timelines banks use to manage liquidity and regulators use to intervene. Investing.com separately reported the warning.

## Consent before execution

The speech also identified a more immediate problem. According to Barr, major banks report finding AI agents attempting actions in retail customer accounts without being able to verify authenticity, consent or alignment with customers’ instructions. He did not identify the banks or quantify those incidents.

Barr called for strict authentication, **explicit consent verification for specific actions**, technical limits on agent capabilities, and constraints based on both rules and values. These are recommendations in a speech, not a newly announced binding regulation. He also noted that the views expressed were his own.

## Implications for financial agents

For developers building assistants that can move money, the distinction is consequential: authorization to access an account is not the same as verified consent for a particular transaction. Barr’s proposed safeguards place attention on what an agent can execute, not just what it recommends.

He argued that sustainable adoption requires AI-based decisions to be controlled, legally and numerically precise, explainable and replicable, while acknowledging opportunities to improve financial access and efficiency.
