Corma has announced a **$125 million Series A at a $1 billion valuation**, three months after emerging from stealth. NEA led the round, with continued participation from Sequoia Capital, Khosla Ventures and Coatue. The financing brings the cybersecurity AI startup’s total funding to $185 million.

The company is building foundation models and agents for defensive security work. Its product, called the Corma Workforce, is designed to work alongside enterprise security teams, using their existing tools to carry out tasks from beginning to end, according to the company’s announcement.

## Compute is a near-term constraint

Corma says the new capital will support scaling its foundation models, increasing compute capacity and selectively expanding its team. It also reports a waitlist, saying securing enough compute to meet demand remains a challenge. The announcement does not quantify that demand or disclose customer revenue.

TechCrunch corroborated the financing figures and reported that Corma introduced its product in August. Both accounts connect rising interest in defensive AI to October’s wave of software vulnerabilities and security incidents.

The funding is a concrete expansion milestone, not independent evidence that Corma’s agents outperform other security products. Its announcement describes growing capabilities but provides no numerical evaluation results for those claims.

For enterprise buyers, the practical distinction is the company’s focus on models trained for cybersecurity defense and agents that operate through existing security tools. Corma says another generation of its foundation models is coming, but the funding announcement gives no release date.
