Google has acquired Crusoe’s data-center site in southeast Wyoming, Crusoe confirmed on November 6. The announcement follows Google’s November 5 commitment to a **long-term $17 billion investment** in its first Wyoming data center, in Laramie County.

The investment figure is not a disclosed acquisition price. TechCrunch reported that Google did not reveal what it paid Crusoe for the unfinished project.

## From site preparation to development

Crusoe said the transaction completes its pre-development role, covering site selection, government approvals and power strategy. It developed the campus plan with Tallgrass Energy, coordinating energy infrastructure and data-center engineering.

The sale does not end the companies’ broader relationship. Crusoe says it remains the developer of Google’s campus under construction in Armstrong County, near Amarillo, Texas.

## Power plans remain central

Google says the Wyoming project is contracted with American Electric Power for a **702-megawatt fuel-cell development** using Bloom Energy equipment, paired with carbon capture and storage. The fuel cells will use natural gas; Google says captured emissions will be stored at Tallgrass’s Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub.

Additional power will come from gas generators operated by Tallgrass and Black Hills Energy. A separate engineering study will assess adding carbon capture to Tallgrass’s combined-cycle turbines. Google also says the data center will use air cooling to reduce pressure on local water resources.

The distinction matters for AI infrastructure: this is a site acquisition and planned buildout, not an announcement that new computing capacity is already operating. The fuel-cell and carbon-capture components are development commitments, not evidence of delivered emissions reductions.
