Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky said his X account was hacked after a series of now-deleted posts promoted real-world asset tokenization, according to multiple crypto news reports published Friday.

The posts described tokenization as a way to divide, trade and settle assets such as buildings, bonds and funds. They quickly drew attention because the tone appeared unlike Chesky's usual posts and because several users criticized the thread as AI-generated "slop." Chesky later said the account had been compromised.

The incident is small compared with protocol exploits or exchange breaches, but it is relevant for crypto security because it shows how account takeovers can blend familiar industry themes with machine-written content. Tokenization is a real market narrative, and public executives often command enough trust to move attention quickly, even when the content is later removed.

For builders, the practical lesson is about source verification rather than the merits of tokenized assets. A polished thread from a known founder account is no longer strong evidence by itself, especially when the post is unusually broad, has no product announcement behind it, or appears during a period of heightened crypto attention.

The reports did not describe any Airbnb product launch, token plan, customer impact, or confirmed financial loss tied to the posts. Based on the available reporting, the conservative read is that this was an X account compromise that used AI-styled crypto messaging, not an Airbnb Web3 initiative.
