Dunamu, the operator of South Korean crypto exchange Upbit, and Visa have outlined a strategic partnership focused on stablecoin payments, cross-border remittances, and AI-related commerce infrastructure.

The companies presented the roadmap this week at Visa's Global Market Support Center in San Francisco. The verified materials describe the agreement as exploratory: Dunamu would bring digital-asset operations and blockchain infrastructure experience, while Visa would bring its global payment and money-movement network.

That distinction matters because the announcement is not a product launch. The companies have not named a live payment service, a target launch date, a blockchain network, a custody provider, or a settlement structure. Korean coverage based on Dunamu's statement says the work will be developed in stages and shaped by regulation, technical feasibility, and business review.

The partnership also includes possible work around Open USD, or OUSD, an open-standard stablecoin model, plus AI-powered payment services. The companies are considering agentic commerce use cases in which AI systems help search, select, buy, and pay for goods or services under user controls.

For South Korea, the useful signal is institutional rather than retail. Large payment networks and domestic crypto infrastructure providers are testing how stablecoins might connect with existing financial rails, but the practical path still depends on compliance, interoperability, and local rules for digital-asset payments and remittances.
