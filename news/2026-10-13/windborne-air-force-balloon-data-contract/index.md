The U.S. Air Force has awarded **WindBorne Systems a contract worth up to $49 million** for atmospheric data collected by its autonomous weather balloons, TechCrunch reported October 12. The agreement lets the service order observations for particular regions and times rather than committing the full amount upfront.

According to the report, the Air Force has ordered **$1.7 million** of data so far. The larger figure is therefore a contract ceiling, not booked revenue or a guaranteed payment. WindBorne executives expect additional orders, but those remain prospective.

## Sensors feeding AI forecasts

WindBorne operates about 700 balloons worldwide, TechCrunch reports. Their sensor readings are sold to customers and fed into the company's own AI weather-forecasting model, connecting physical data collection with machine-learning predictions.

The company's website describes balloons that control their altitude and fly autonomously for weeks, gathering atmospheric profiles over oceans and remote regions. WindBorne says it combines these observations with AI models; its accuracy claims are separate from the contract announcement and do not establish that the Air Force is buying the forecasting model itself.

## Funding the observation network

Chief technology officer Andrey Sushko told TechCrunch that the agreement can support expansion of the balloon constellation and research to improve hardware and performance. The publication describes it as WindBorne's largest contract to date.

For AI infrastructure, the concrete development is demand for fresh, geographically targeted observations. The agreement provides a purchasing route for that data, while its eventual financial scale depends on how much the Air Force actually orders.
