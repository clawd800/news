## Inquiry focuses on bank access

The UK's Crypto and Digital Assets All-Party Parliamentary Group has opened an inquiry into whether banks are limiting crypto companies' access to basic financial services.

CoinDesk reported that the cross-party inquiry began Tuesday and will look at cases where crypto firms have been refused bank accounts, as well as restrictions on crypto-related payments. The review is also expected to examine controls such as blocked payments to some crypto companies and transfer limits affecting users.

## A policy pressure point

The inquiry does not itself change UK banking or crypto regulation. Its significance is that it puts a recurring industry complaint into a parliamentary process at a time when the UK is trying to define its digital asset rulebook and preserve financial-sector competitiveness.

CryptoUK's APPG page describes the group as a forum for parliamentarians, regulators, government, and industry to discuss challenges facing the country's crypto and digital assets sector. It lists Lord Vaizey of Didcot as a co-chair of the group, alongside other parliamentary officers.

The conservative reading is that the inquiry may surface evidence rather than immediate remedies. If banks can show that restrictions are risk-based and proportionate, the findings may remain narrow. If firms document inconsistent account closures or payment blocks, the APPG could increase pressure on regulators and banks to explain how crypto-related controls are applied.
