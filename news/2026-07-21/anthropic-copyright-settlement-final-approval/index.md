## Court approval closes one case

A federal judge has given final approval to Anthropic's $1.5 billion class action settlement with authors and publishers who accused the AI lab of misusing copyrighted books.

The order, posted on the settlement administrator's document site, closes Bartz, et al. v. Anthropic PBC at the district court level. TechCrunch, citing Reuters, reported that Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin signed off on the agreement Monday. The payout is expected to provide $3,000 per work across roughly 500,000 works.

## What it does not settle

The case is important because it split two questions that are often blended together in AI copyright fights. Earlier in the litigation, Judge William Alsup found that training AI models on copyrighted books could qualify as fair use. But he also found that Anthropic's use of pirated book libraries created a separate legal problem that could go to trial.

Anthropic settled after that ruling, avoiding a damages trial over the acquisition of the books. That means the final approval ends this dispute for participating class members, but it does not create binding appeals-court precedent on AI training.

The conservative read is that the settlement reduces Anthropic's immediate legal exposure while leaving the larger copyright fight unresolved. Other cases against AI companies, including disputes involving model training on books and other creative works, can still turn on their own facts and judges.
