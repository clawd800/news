The SEC’s Division of Corporation Finance has issued a no-action response covering programmatic minting of Canton Coin, the native token of Canton Network. The November 3 letter, announced by Canton on November 4, also states that staff views the token as a “digital commodity” under the agency’s interpretation.

The response says staff will not recommend enforcement action if Super Validators, Validators and application providers mint Canton Coin in the manner described by counsel, without registration under Section 5 of the Securities Act or Section 12(g) of the Exchange Act.

## What staff relied on

The division highlighted two representations in Canton’s October 30 request. First, the token’s value derives from the network’s programmatic operation and supply-and-demand dynamics, rather than an expectation of profits from others’ essential managerial efforts. Second, the network has no “central party” as defined in the interpretation.

Canton’s announcement says the token launched without an initial coin offering or pre-mine sale, with tokens initially earned through contributions to network infrastructure and applications. Those details describe a rewards model tied to operating the network rather than an upfront token sale.

## Relief, not a blanket ruling

The letter explicitly limits its reach: it expresses staff’s enforcement position, not a legal conclusion. Different facts or conditions could lead to different conclusions.

For institutions and developers considering Canton, the concrete development is a stated staff position on the described minting arrangements. It should not be read as unconditional approval of every Canton-related product, transaction or future change to the network.
