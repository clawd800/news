Morgan Stanley says it is converting U.S. equities and U.S. Treasuries into tokenized form through the **DTCC Tokenization Service**, providing an early indication of bank use after the platform's October 28 launch.

In a November 3 announcement on X, the bank described itself as among the first banks to tokenize real-world assets through the service. It did not identify individual securities, transaction amounts or the blockchain used. Unchained reported that the announcement also left unclear whether the tokens were held for clients or for Morgan Stanley itself.

## From access agreement to reported use

DTCC's launch release listed Morgan Stanley among 20 DTC participants that had signed agreements to access the service. Signing an agreement does not establish that a participant has tokenized assets; the bank's subsequent statement adds that distinction, though it does not establish the scale of activity.

The service creates tokenized representations of securities already held at The Depository Trust Company. DTCC calls these representations digital twins and says they retain the ownership rights, investor protections and economic entitlements of the traditional securities. Eligible users can convert securities entitlements between traditional and tokenized forms.

DTCC says the service currently integrates with Canton Network and its private Besu network. That platform-level support should not be read as confirmation of which network Morgan Stanley used.

The development is a concrete adoption signal for institutional tokenization infrastructure, but the public disclosure remains limited. It supplies no basis for estimating tokenized assets, trading volume or a broader customer rollout.
