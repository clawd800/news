Palo Alto Networks is in talks to acquire **Clutch Security** for $500 million in cash, plus additional milestone-based payments, TechCrunch reported on November 5, citing a person familiar with the discussions. The reported negotiations concern a startup focused on securing AI agents' access to corporate systems.

**This is not an announced acquisition.** TechCrunch said neither company responded to its request for comment and credited The Information with first reporting the discussions. The purchase price and additional payments remain reported terms, not confirmed commitments from either company.

## What Clutch Secures

Clutch's own website describes a platform covering enterprise identities, AI agents, and secrets. It says the service inventories those assets, contextualizes findings, assigns risk scores, and recommends actions. Its advertised connections span cloud providers, SaaS applications, vaults, code repositories, and endpoints.

That product scope makes the reported target relevant to the infrastructure around agents, rather than to model development itself. The focus is on discovering and governing the identities and credentials through which software accesses enterprise resources.

## Reported Deal Economics

According to TechCrunch's source, Clutch was last valued at $85 million when it raised a $20 million Series A in January 2025 and is approaching $10 million in annual recurring revenue. Those private-company figures have not been independently confirmed here.

If completed on the reported terms, the transaction would bring that identity-and-agent security capability into a much larger cybersecurity vendor. For now, whether the talks produce a signed agreement remains unresolved.
