Apple has agreed to obtain a **non-exclusive license to Huxe's intellectual property** and the right to make employment offers to certain employees of the AI audio startup, according to October 9–10 reporting by MacRumors and TechCrunch citing a European Commission filing.

The arrangement is a hiring-and-licensing deal, not an outright acquisition of Huxe. Both reports say Apple notified the Commission on June 9. The news is the newly reported disclosure, rather than an agreement announced this weekend.

## What Huxe built

Huxe developed personalized AI audio, including briefings and podcast-style explanations of topics chosen by listeners. Its website describes an interactive listening experience in which users could interrupt to request a different explanation or more technical detail. MacRumors reports that the startup was founded by former members of Google's NotebookLM team.

Huxe's own wind-down notice says it stopped development and removed its app from the App Store and Play Store on May 21. Service ended on May 28, with permanent deletion of user data scheduled for May 29.

## What remains unknown

The reported filing does not identify the employees covered by the arrangement or confirm that anyone accepted an offer. It also does not disclose Apple's intended use of the technology.

That distinction matters: access to audio-generation intellectual property and potential recruits is not evidence of an upcoming Apple Podcasts feature. For now, the concrete development is Apple's agreement to license technology from a shuttered AI audio startup while securing a route to recruit some of its staff.
