Broadridge announced November 2 that it plans to provide **on-chain shareholder governance and digital transfer agency services** for the New York Stock Exchange's proposed tokenized securities platform. The platform remains subject to regulatory approval; this is an infrastructure announcement, not the start of live tokenized stock trading.

The planned integration addresses what happens after an investor acquires a tokenized share. NYSE will use Broadridge's governance smart contracts to connect securities with meeting agendas, voting rules, and verifiable results. The framework also supports distribution of proxy materials and authentication of digital wallets against verified voting entitlements.

Broadridge says investors will be able to participate through supported wallets, broker interfaces, or its ProxyVote service. That design would let shareholder voting connect to blockchain-based ownership without requiring every investor to use the same interface.

The transfer agency component is intended to let NYSE-listed issuers tokenize their stock for trading on the planned platform. Broadridge is bringing digital and traditional transfer agency services together with proxy voting, linking issuer records and shareholder communications to the trading infrastructure.

The concrete development is the proposed connection between token ownership and corporate governance. Moving a share onto a blockchain does not, by itself, establish how its holder receives meeting information or proves voting eligibility. Broadridge's announced role targets those operational requirements.

Execution remains the key test. The announcement describes the intended capabilities, but regulatory approval and implementation must precede their use on NYSE's tokenized securities venue.
