Apple's **Audio Intelligence** features for Apple Watch are entering developer beta testing through the iOS 27.3 update, TechCrunch reported on November 2. The rollout moves conversation-recall tools announced in September into testing, rather than general availability.

The two central features serve different purposes. **Live Rewind** transcribes the preceding 15 seconds of speech when a user double-clicks the Digital Crown. Apple's product documentation says users can ask Siri about the transcript or save it in the Siri app for later reference.

**Siri Recap** turns conversations into high-level notes containing a title, summary, and key points. Apple says users can review those notes on their watch or iPhone, switch the feature off through Control Center, or choose when and where it operates. It is not presented as a verbatim meeting-transcription service.

Privacy is a central part of Apple's stated design. The company says the features do not create or store audio recordings, delete raw audio after processing, and do not attribute speech to individual speakers. Saved transcripts and recaps are end-to-end encrypted during iCloud syncing. Those are Apple's design claims, not an independent security assessment.

TechCrunch identifies Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 as supported hardware and reports that processing combines the watch, a paired iPhone, and Private Cloud Compute. The features therefore should not be understood as entirely watch-local AI.

Apple's public product page still labels Live Rewind and Siri Recap as coming in beta in late 2026 and warns that usage limits may apply. Developer testing is the immediate milestone; broad consumer availability remains separate.
