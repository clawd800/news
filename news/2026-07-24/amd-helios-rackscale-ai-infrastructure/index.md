## AMD moves from chips to racks

AMD used its Advancing AI 2026 event to launch **Helios**, a rackscale AI system that packages AMD Instinct GPUs, EPYC CPUs, Pensando networking, and ROCm software into a full data-center building block. The company is positioning Helios as a more complete answer to Nvidia's rack-level systems, not just another accelerator card.

The announcement matters because frontier AI buyers increasingly purchase at the rack and cluster level. AMD says Helios is already in production for gigawatt-scale deployments and claims the system can deliver up to 30% more inference tokens per dollar than competing racks. That performance claim comes from AMD, so it should be treated as a vendor benchmark until independent results appear.

## Anthropic and OpenAI are early anchors

The most concrete customer detail is Anthropic's separate agreement to deploy up to **2 gigawatts** of AMD Instinct MI450 Series GPUs in Helios rackscale systems. AMD said the first gigawatt is expected to begin deployment in the first half of 2027, and that Anthropic will use MI455X GPUs, EPYC "Venice" CPUs, Pensando networking, and ROCm software.

OpenAI is also named as a Helios partner. AMD said OpenAI expects to bring Helios online beginning in the fourth quarter of 2026, with deployments accelerating through 2027, while Meta has begun testing and validating workloads on Helios racks.

The conservative read is that AMD is trying to narrow Nvidia's platform advantage by selling a full stack, not only faster chips. The open question is whether ROCm, networking, and customer software work can mature quickly enough for hyperscale buyers to treat Helios as a repeatable alternative.
