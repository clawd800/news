The U.S. Senate failed to advance the Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act on September 15, dealing a setback to legislation intended to establish federal rules for cryptocurrency markets. CoinDesk reported a **49–50 procedural vote**, short of the 60 votes needed to move forward.

The result blocks this attempt to advance the bill; it is not a new restriction on crypto trading or a repeal of existing law. The proposal seeks to clarify how digital assets and blockchain projects are regulated, including the respective responsibilities of the Securities and Exchange Commission and Commodity Futures Trading Commission.

## Ethics and implementation disputes

In a public statement, Senator Elissa Slotkin confirmed that she voted against the legislation, calling its ethics provisions “simply too thin.” She argued that stronger safeguards were needed around public officials’ crypto business interests.

Slotkin also cited concerns about anti-money-laundering protections and the CFTC’s staffing and oversight capacity. She said the bill contained bipartisan provisions that could support another attempt, leaving open the possibility of further negotiations rather than rejecting a regulatory framework altogether.

## What changes for builders

For exchanges, token issuers and blockchain infrastructure developers, the immediate consequence is continued uncertainty about a statutory market-structure framework. CoinDesk reported that attention would shift toward ongoing SEC and CFTC initiatives, although agency action is distinct from congressional legislation.

The failed procedural vote does not establish that the bill can never return. It does mean that businesses cannot treat its proposed rules as enacted law or plan around a confirmed implementation date.
