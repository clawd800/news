Rivian announced on October 22 that it has acquired **Third Dimension AI**, bringing a platform for generating controllable 3D environments into its autonomous-driving program. Financial terms were not disclosed.

The startup converts real-world data into detailed virtual environments where developers can recreate driving situations and vary their conditions. Rivian says the technology will support synthetic-data generation and simulation for both driver-assistance features and self-driving systems.

## Turning road data into repeatable tests

Simulation lets developers revisit difficult situations without waiting for the same event to happen again on public roads. Rivian’s autonomy and AI chief, James Philbin, said Third Dimension’s technology will help the company recreate challenging scenarios, change conditions and improve system responses across its consumer-vehicle and robotaxi programs.

TechCrunch reported that the startup’s 15-person team, mostly based in the United Kingdom, will join Rivian’s autonomy and AI organization. The acquisition also gives Rivian an opportunity to expand its London AI hub, Philbin told the publication.

Third Dimension was founded in 2024 by Tolga Kart and Piotr Sokolski. Its technology turns images and videos into lifelike 3D worlds, with applications extending beyond its initial work on tools for video-game development.

## Infrastructure, not a self-driving launch

The transaction adds a training and validation capability rather than announcing a new autonomous-driving service. Rivian’s stated goal is to combine reconstructed environments with its driving data so engineers can generate variations and rare edge cases at scale.

Neither the acquisition announcement nor the reported interviews establishes measured safety improvements from the integration. Those outcomes remain to be demonstrated as Rivian incorporates the platform into its development workflow.
