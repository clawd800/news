Crusoe and Boom Supersonic are **no longer launching Boom’s Superpower turbine together**, ending a planned supply relationship intended to bring natural-gas generation to AI data centers. Boom CEO Blake Scholl confirmed the change publicly, and Crusoe separately confirmed the split to TechCrunch in its September 25 report.

The original December 2025 announcement named Crusoe as Superpower’s launch customer, with an order for **29 turbines totaling 1.21 gigawatts**. Boom described a backlog of more than $1.25 billion for the product. Each Superpower unit was designed for 42 megawatts of output; initial deliveries under the arrangement had been expected in 2027, according to TechCrunch.

## A change in customer, not a product shutdown

Scholl said Boom expects to deliver approximately 250 megawatts of Superpower capacity to other sites next year and is targeting one gigawatt in 2028. Those figures remain company projections, not completed installations. His statement did not identify the replacement customers.

Crusoe told TechCrunch that the partnership was no longer the right fit and that it selects energy sources according to each site’s needs. Its spokesperson included turbines alongside wind, solar, batteries and grid power, so the split should not be read as a company-wide rejection of gas generation.

For AI infrastructure, the development illustrates the gap between announced power-supply agreements and equipment actually serving a campus. Boom loses its announced launch customer, while Crusoe retains flexibility over its energy mix. The public statements do not establish cancellation fees or other financial settlement terms.
