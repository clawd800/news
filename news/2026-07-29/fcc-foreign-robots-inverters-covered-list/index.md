The Federal Communications Commission has updated its **Covered List** to include foreign-produced advanced robotic devices and power inverters, extending a national security tool previously associated with telecom and networking equipment into robotics and energy infrastructure.

The FCC said the update follows determinations from executive branch agencies that the devices can pose national security risks. The category covers mobile robotics such as humanoid and quadruped systems, but the scope is broader than legged machines. The agency's FAQ describes an advanced robotic device as a machine with ground movement, environmental sensing, and network connectivity, subject to size and configuration details.

Power inverters are also included because they sit inside solar, battery, and other energy systems that increasingly connect to software-managed grids. That makes the rule relevant beyond consumer devices: the affected equipment touches factory automation, warehouse robotics, renewable energy deployments, and potentially AI infrastructure projects that depend on reliable power systems.

The practical effect is a new authorization barrier for products that fall under the Covered List framework. Companies seeking to continue importing or marketing covered equipment in the United States may need to pursue waivers or other approvals rather than relying on standard device authorization.

The move is also another sign that physical AI is being treated as infrastructure, not just hardware. Robotics policy is now intersecting with supply chain security, grid resilience, and connected-device risk in the same regulatory lane as routers and communications gear.
