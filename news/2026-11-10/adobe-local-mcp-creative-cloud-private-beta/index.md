Adobe previewed **local Model Context Protocol integrations** for Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere and After Effects at its MAX conference on November 10. The integrations will let users direct third-party AI agents to modify native project files on their computers. Adobe says a **private beta is planned for early 2027**, rather than general availability today.

The proposed connection goes beyond generating an image or returning editing instructions. Adobe says agents will work inside Creative Cloud applications while keeping layers, vectors, bins and sequences editable. Users can intervene and refine the work manually instead of receiving only a flattened output.

Adobe also describes workflows spanning other MCP-connected services. An agent connected to the relevant tools could retrieve a brief from Google Docs, work on a project using Adobe applications and share the result through Slack. Those examples depend on the agent having the additional connections; they are not a claim that every integration ships automatically.

The scope of exposed functionality is still evolving. Adobe vice president Deepa Subramaniam told TechCrunch that the company is evaluating which capabilities to expose and will use feedback to guide that process.

Separately, Adobe is expanding its own Creative Agent across desktop applications. Its AI Assistants are available in beta, with Photoshop's assistant offered in the Photoshop beta app. Premiere capabilities include applying effects across clips and adjusting color and exposure across a sequence.

For agent developers, the distinction is important: Adobe's built-in assistants and its forthcoming third-party MCP access have different release timelines. The announcement establishes a planned route into editable creative projects, not an already generally available automation interface.
