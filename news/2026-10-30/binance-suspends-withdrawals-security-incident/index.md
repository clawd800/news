Binance temporarily suspended withdrawals on multiple networks on October 29 after a security incident, according to a company spokesperson quoted by TechCrunch. The exchange said it was investigating reports of a potential security issue and described the suspension as a precautionary measure.

The immediate confirmed development is the withdrawal interruption, not a settled accounting of losses. Binance told TechCrunch that user funds were safe, while saying its security teams were urgently reviewing the available information. The company also warned users against unofficial links, messages and requests for account information.

## Stablecoin freezes enter the response

Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino said in a public post that Tether had frozen more than **1.567 billion USDT** related to a potential Binance exchange hack. That is an issuer-reported freeze amount, not a verified measure of Binance's final loss or money already returned to customers.

Binance founder Changpeng Zhao separately said the incident appeared to be a hack. He stated that he no longer manages Binance and, speaking as a shareholder, expected user funds to be fully covered. That expectation is distinct from a detailed compensation plan issued by the exchange.

## What remains unresolved

TechCrunch reported that the attackers' identity was unclear. Estimates of the amount drained were still circulating, so they should not be treated as an established loss total.

The incident puts exchange custody and stablecoin issuers' emergency controls in focus. Freezing assets can restrict their movement, but does not by itself establish recovery, customer reimbursement or when withdrawals can safely resume.
