Snap has introduced **Specs Intelligence**, a personal AI service designed for iPhone, Mac, and its Specs augmented-reality glasses. An iOS preview is available to U.S. adults without an invitation, while the fuller early-access experience is limited to invited Mac users through a waitlist.

The distinction matters: Snap says the mobile preview connects to a Google account and uses Gmail and Google Calendar to provide insights into where users spend their time and attention. **It does not include the broader service's anticipatory suggestions or actions.**

## Beyond a chat window

Snap describes the broader service as building personal context from apps and tools users choose to connect. Intended examples include preparing questions before a meeting, collecting flight and hotel details, and flagging a work deadline that overlaps a trip.

Features called Corners organize areas such as work and family, while Goals track longer-term priorities. The company says this context is designed to carry across devices. Glasses are not required to use the service, although Snap envisions displaying relevant travel information and daily briefings in the wearer's field of view.

## Access and data boundaries

Snap says users control which services they connect. It states that personnel cannot view or access personal content used with Specs Intelligence, and that content from connected accounts will not train or fine-tune its AI models or support personalized advertising. Processing combines on-device computation with cloud infrastructure.

Those are company commitments, not independently demonstrated privacy guarantees. For prospective users, the immediate product is narrower than the launch's proactive-assistant vision: mobile account insights now, with the fuller Mac experience gated by invitations.
