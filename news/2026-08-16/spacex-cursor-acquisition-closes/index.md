Cursor says it is moving ahead "together with SpaceX," marking a major ownership shift for one of the most visible AI coding tools. TechCrunch reported that SpaceX has officially closed its acquisition of Cursor, and Cursor's own announcement frames the move around access to large-scale compute.

In the post, Cursor says better models have pushed the product from code completion toward "AI teammates" that can be assigned real work. The company says SpaceX will give it access to what it calls the largest fleet of GPUs in the world, with the goal of building stronger models that are cheaper to run for customers.

The deal matters because AI coding products increasingly depend on both model quality and inference economics. Cursor has been one of the main developer-facing examples of that pressure: better agents require more compute, while developers still expect fast responses and predictable prices.

Cursor also points to Grok 4.6 as an early example of what the companies can build together, saying SpaceX is building the capacity needed to scale intelligence. That makes the transaction more than a software acquisition. It ties a popular coding interface to a broader compute strategy controlled by Elon Musk’s companies.

Neither source adds new terms for the transaction in the latest announcement, so the clearest verified development is the closing itself and Cursor’s stated plan to use SpaceX infrastructure for future coding models.
