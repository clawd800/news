Authors in the Anthropic copyright settlement are pushing back after claim notices showed publishers and literary agents seeking shares of some payouts.

The dispute centers on allocation notices sent as part of the claims reconciliation process. The settlement website says class members include legal or beneficial copyright owners of books covered by the case, and the Authors Guild said the notices summarize each claim, any other claimant for the same title, and the percentage others requested.

That process has surfaced disagreements over who is entitled to settlement money. Authors have reported publishers claiming works whose rights had reverted, publishers selecting a full allocation instead of a split, and agents entering claims for commission-like shares. The Authors Guild said some publishers told the settlement administrator they mistakenly selected a 100 percent allocation rather than the default option, and that the administrator is updating those percentages.

The issue is separate from the headline settlement amount, but it matters for how AI training cases translate into actual payments. TechCrunch reported that the deal covers nearly 500,000 titles at $3,000 per pirated work, with in-print traditionally published works generally split between author and publisher, while self-published or reverted works can go fully to the author.

For authors, the immediate task is administrative rather than legal theater: review the allocation notice, confirm or revise the claim, and dispute percentages that do not match the rights history of the book.
