LG Electronics and NVIDIA have demonstrated **CLOiD performing robotic manipulation and manufacturing tasks in an actual manufacturing environment**, according to a new NVIDIA update. The robot used NVIDIA's Isaac GR00T technology, moving the collaboration beyond its earlier description of exploring the model for LG robots.

The reported milestone is a demonstration, not an announcement of a commercial factory rollout. NVIDIA does not specify the factory, individual tasks, success rates, cycle times, or the number of robots involved.

## A connected training workflow

The companies implemented a development loop combining real-world data collection, NVIDIA Cosmos-based data augmentation, GR00T model training, Jetson AGX Thor onboard computing, and simulation-based evaluation.

NVIDIA says the resulting workflows are reusable for continued development and validation of CLOiD's capabilities. The teams are using the work to refine a repeatable process for training, evaluating, and improving manufacturing robots.

That makes the engineering workflow as important as the demonstration itself: the announcement describes how data, training, onboard computing, and evaluation connect, rather than presenting only a robot performing an isolated task.

## From plans to a reported demonstration

In June, NVIDIA described LG Electronics as exploring GR00T for home robots and modular robotics platforms. It also outlined plans for simulation-based development and Cosmos-generated training data. The latest update identifies a specific robot and a manufacturing-environment demonstration built around those technologies.

The evidence remains company-reported. Without published task-level results or deployment figures, it does not establish production reliability or economic gains. It does, however, provide a concrete follow-up to the earlier robotics roadmap.
