South Korea's Ministry of Economy and Finance says it will begin a **fourth-quarter pilot** that uses **blockchain-based deposit tokens** for some government spending, extending the country's real-world testing of tokenized money beyond consumer-facing payments.

## What changes

According to the ministry announcement cited by local media on April 16, the project was selected for South Korea's **2026 regulatory sandbox**. The first use case is expected to cover certain **business promotion expenses**, which are currently paid through government purchasing cards under the Treasury Funds Management Act.

Under the sandbox, agencies can test a different payment rail on a limited basis. The ministry says tokenized deposits can be programmed with conditions such as **when funds may be spent** and **which merchant categories can accept them**, giving officials tighter control than a standard card-based workflow.

## Why it matters

This is a narrow public-finance pilot, not a general launch of retail CBDC payments. That distinction matters. The conservative read is that Seoul is testing whether tokenized bank money can make treasury operations more transparent and cheaper to run, especially by reducing card-network intermediation and some of the manual review now triggered by off-hours spending.

The plan also follows an earlier treasury-related pilot tied to **EV charging infrastructure subsidies**, suggesting South Korea is moving deposit tokens from theory toward specific fiscal workflows. If the Sejong-based test works as intended, the government says it will consider expanding the model more broadly.
