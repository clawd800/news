Aave's rsETH response has moved from general relief pledges to a more concrete funding stack, with governance proposals now defining how much capital could come from the protocol itself and from allied ecosystems.

## What changed

An Aave governance proposal posted April 24 asks the DAO to contribute **25,000 ETH** to the coordinated **DeFi United** recovery effort. The proposal says the April 18 exploit created an original shortfall of about **163,183 ETH** and that, after freezes and expected liquidations, the remaining funding gap is roughly **75,081 ETH**.

The same proposal says public commitments from contributors including EtherFi, Lido, Ethena, Ink, BGD Labs, Ernesto, Emilio, and Stani Kulechov total **14,570 ETH** so far. Separately, a Mantle forum discussion proposes a credit facility of up to **30,000 ETH** for the effort.

A parallel proposal on Arbitrum asks governance to release **30,765.67 ETH** frozen by the network's Security Council after the exploit and route those funds into the remediation plan.

## Why it matters

The key shift is that the recovery is no longer framed only as emergency coordination. It is becoming a multi-governance capital plan with identifiable treasury asks, pending votes, and a clearer path to restoring rsETH backing.

That still leaves execution risk. Several pieces, including the Arbitrum release and treasury approvals, remain subject to governance outcomes rather than being immediately deployable.
