QuTwo, the Finnish AI lab founded by former Silo AI CEO **Peter Sarlin**, has reached a **€325 million valuation** after a **€25 million angel round**, according to separate reports from **TechCrunch** and **The Next Web**. Both outlets describe the financing as an unusual structure for a company only months out of stealth, with Sarlin favoring angel backing over a conventional VC-led round.

## What QuTwo is building

On its official site, QuTwo says **Qutwo OS** is an orchestration layer for **classical AI, hybrid, and full quantum systems**. The company is not selling quantum hardware itself. Instead, it says it is helping enterprises route workloads across classical infrastructure today while preparing for future quantum environments.

That product framing matters because the funding story is really an infrastructure bet. TechCrunch and TNW both describe QuTwo as trying to build the software layer between current enterprise AI systems and later quantum compute, rather than waiting for quantum hardware to mature before pursuing commercial work.

## Why it matters

The conservative takeaway is not that useful large-scale quantum computing is already here. It is that investors are assigning a meaningful valuation to software meant to manage that transition layer early. Sarlin's track record also matters: he previously built **Silo AI**, which **AMD acquired in 2024**.

For AI infrastructure, QuTwo is a notable signal that investors see orchestration and workload routing as a strategic category of its own, even before quantum hardware is broadly usable.
