Tether says it generated **$1.04 billion** in net profit in the first quarter of 2026 and ended March with **$8.23 billion** in excess reserves backing **USDT**, according to a quarterly attestation published Friday. The filing, prepared by **BDO**, put total assets at **$191.77 billion** against **$183.54 billion** in liabilities, including **$183.44 billion** tied to issued digital tokens.

## What the filing shows

The report says Tether's reserves remain concentrated in highly liquid instruments, with Treasury-backed exposure still doing most of the work. Tether separately said direct and indirect exposure to U.S. Treasuries was about **$141 billion** at quarter end. The attested reserve breakdown also listed roughly **$19.84 billion** in physical gold and about **$6.62 billion** in bitcoin.

USDT in circulation was broadly stable during the quarter rather than surging, which makes the larger reserve buffer the more notable change. In other words, the company is claiming a thicker capital cushion without leaning on a big wave of new issuance to get there.

## Why it matters

Stablecoin disclosures matter more now that dollar tokens are increasingly used for payments, settlement, and treasury operations instead of just crypto trading. Tether is still publishing an attestation rather than a full audit, so the document is narrower and reflects the balance sheet at a specific date. But for the world's largest stablecoin issuer, an **$8.23 billion** excess reserve figure is still a meaningful data point as regulators and institutions keep pressing on reserve quality, liquidity, and transparency.
