Amazon and Meta have held multiple rounds of talks about allowing Meta's **Muse AI agent** to connect to Amazon's store, Fortune reported on November 25, citing people familiar with the discussions. A deal could end restrictions Amazon imposed in September, but the negotiations remain unresolved.

The central issues include how product search results would appear and how advertising would work. Fortune reports that Amazon is concerned about lost advertising revenue, the quality of results surfaced by Muse, and whether outside agents could weaken its direct relationship with shoppers.

## Talks are not restored access

Neither company has announced an agreement. Fortune says both declined to comment and that any deal is expected to need approval from both companies' chief executives. The report does not establish a timetable or confirm how Muse would technically connect to Amazon.

TechCrunch also covered the discussions, explicitly citing Fortune. That provides a second published account, but not independent confirmation of the negotiations.

The reported talks follow Amazon's September blockade of Muse, which prevented users from completing shopping tasks on the retailer's site. Negotiating access would represent a possible change in that relationship, not evidence that the restrictions have already been lifted.

## Commercial terms shape agent access

For developers building purchasing agents, the issue extends beyond whether software can navigate a store. Retailers also control access and have commercial interests in product discovery, advertising and customer relationships.

The concrete development is a reported negotiation over those terms. There is no newly announced integration, public API or confirmed restoration of Muse checkout access in either account.
