President Trump used a White House meeting with crypto and finance executives on Wednesday to renew pressure on Congress to advance the Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act, the market-structure bill that would set clearer federal rules for digital assets.

The push matters because the legislation is aimed at one of the industry's longest-running policy fights: which digital assets are overseen by securities regulators, which fall under commodities rules, and how trading platforms should register. The bill has drawn attention from exchanges, token issuers, custodians, and stablecoin companies that want a more predictable U.S. framework.

House Financial Services Committee leaders said in May that the House had passed the CLARITY Act with support from 216 Republicans and 78 Democrats, and that the Senate Banking Committee had advanced its own work on the bill. That leaves the Senate floor as the next major test.

Trump's remarks do not make passage certain. Senate timing remains uncertain, and lawmakers still need to reconcile competing priorities around investor protection, agency authority, and how much discretion regulators should keep. But the White House event puts market-structure legislation back in the foreground after this year's stablecoin law, signaling that crypto policy remains part of the administration's broader financial technology agenda.
