Google Labs announced on September 17 that **CC is expanding into a household coordination agent**, moving beyond its original personal daily briefing. The experiment gives CC its own Google account and lets up to six members share information and collaborate with it.

Each member chooses what CC can access. Users can automatically share messages from selected email senders, forward individual items, or provide files, calendars and information through Google Chat. Google says CC only responds to group members and will not act or share information outside the group without permission.

## From briefings to shared tasks

CC produces a shared “Your Day Ahead” briefing and can extract dates and to-dos into a household Calendar or Tasks list. Google describes additional capabilities including pre-filling activity registration PDFs, preparing school-supply shopping lists, planning meals and checking travel times between activities.

The agent asks for missing details and maintains shared memory, distinguishing household-wide information from individual preferences. Google says each CC runs on an isolated cloud computer powered by Gemini models and its Antigravity agent framework, with connections to familiar tools including Gmail, Docs and Calendar.

The notable change is the coordination model: several people can selectively contribute context to one agent with a separate identity, rather than keeping every task inside one person's assistant session. Whether that reduces household work reliably remains unproven by the announcement.

Access is still limited. Google calls CC an early experiment for U.S. adults aged 18 or older with personal Google accounts. Existing users will receive upgrade invitations by email; new users must join a waitlist.
