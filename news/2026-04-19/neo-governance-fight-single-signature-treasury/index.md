Neo's internal governance dispute is moving from a long-running founder feud into a concrete fight over how one of crypto's older treasuries is controlled. The immediate backdrop is the project's FY2025 financial report, which disclosed **$460.8 million** in consolidated assets and said **86% of Neo Foundation assets** were still held in **single-signature wallets**.

## What the two sides want

Da Hongfei's April 9 restructuring proposal argues Neo needs a clean institutional reset. He wants the foundation redomiciled to the Cayman Islands, oversight shifted to an independent five-seat board, and both founders barred from serving on that board for the first 24 months. The stated goal is to move protocol stewardship away from founder control and into a more durable governance structure.

Erik Zhang's counterproposal takes a different route. It centers Neo governance on on-chain verifiable community authorization, tighter constraints over public assets, and stronger long-term accountability mechanisms inside the existing governance framework.

## Why this matters

This is not just a personality clash. Neo's own report says the treasury includes **$224.7 million** at the Neo Foundation and **$236.1 million** at Neo Global Development, while also disclosing **25 single-signature** and **17 multi-signature** wallet addresses. That makes custody design a live governance issue, not a theoretical one.

The conservative takeaway is that Neo now has public numbers, public restructuring plans, and no obvious agreement between its founders on who should control the treasury next.
