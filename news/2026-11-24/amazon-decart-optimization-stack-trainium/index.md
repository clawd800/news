Amazon announced on November 23 that it has signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind the **Decart Optimization Stack (DOS)**. The arrangement also brings Decart's three co-founders and its team of AI researchers and engineers to Amazon.

The transaction is not yet complete. Amazon says closing remains subject to customary conditions, including regulatory clearance. Its announcement describes a technology purchase and team move, rather than an acquisition of all Decart operations.

## Software for custom silicon

DOS is optimization software intended to make AI workloads run more efficiently across hardware. TechCrunch reports that the stack supports Nvidia, Amazon, and Google hardware. Amazon says it began collaborating with Decart in 2025, with the teams taking a full-stack approach spanning software and silicon to improve Trainium performance.

Amazon frames the deal around inference speed, compute efficiency, and its custom-chip investments. The intended benefits cover training, reinforcement learning, and inference, but the announcement does not provide numerical performance benchmarks or a timetable for customer-facing changes.

## Price remains a reported figure

TechCrunch, citing Bloomberg, reports a **$7.5 billion** price. Amazon's own announcement does not disclose financial terms, so that amount should be treated as reported rather than company-confirmed.

For developers evaluating AI infrastructure, the concrete development is Amazon's proposed addition of optimization technology and engineering expertise to its chip efforts. Faster workloads and lower costs remain stated objectives, not newly demonstrated results. The announcement does not introduce a new API, migration tool, or generally available software release.
