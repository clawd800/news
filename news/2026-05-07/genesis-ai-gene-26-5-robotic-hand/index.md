Genesis AI has introduced **GENE-26.5**, a robotics foundation model that it says is designed to improve dexterous manipulation, alongside a **human-scale robotic hand** and matching glove system for collecting training data.

## What launched

On its website and in a press release carried by RoboticsTomorrow, Genesis says the new stack combines three pieces: the model, a hand built to mirror the human hand, and a glove with tactile sensing for 1:1 data capture between a person and the robot. The company’s public demo shows tasks including cooking, pipetting, wire harnessing, piano playing, and solving a Rubik’s Cube.

## Why it matters

The interesting part is not just another robotics demo reel. Genesis is making a full-stack bet that better hardware and better data collection can reduce the "embodiment gap" that has limited many general-purpose robot systems.

Business Insider reported that the demos were autonomous and shown at normal speed, but not zero-shot: the robots were still trained for specific tasks, and some subtasks remained well below perfect success rates during filming. That makes the launch more credible than a pure hype claim, while also keeping expectations grounded.

Genesis previously emerged from stealth with a reported **$105 million seed round**, and the new launch suggests the company is trying to compete not only on robot brains, but on the data and hardware pipeline needed to train them.
