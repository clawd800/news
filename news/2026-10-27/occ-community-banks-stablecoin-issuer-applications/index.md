The U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency on October 26 encouraged community banks to develop payment stablecoin capabilities and consider applying to become permitted issuers, either themselves or through subsidiaries, as applicable.

The regulator linked the invitation to its Community Bank Comeback initiatives, which aim to tailor regulation and supervision for smaller lenders. The announcement encourages participation; it does not itself approve any bank to issue tokens.

## A deadline, with conditional relief

An October 21 OCC bulletin says payment stablecoin issuers in the United States must comply with the GENIUS Act beginning January 18, 2027. Issuance generally requires approval as a permitted payment stablecoin issuer from that date.

Existing issuers with substantially complete applications pending at the OCC by the deadline may request waivers. The agency generally expects to grant relief from the prior-approval requirement for existing U.S. issuers unless its review identifies stronger reasons to refuse. That is not an automatic exemption from other obligations.

Prospective issuers face a different position: the OCC generally expects businesses not already issuing in the United States to wait for approval before starting operations.

## Applications are the next step

The bulletin says application intake will begin after Office of Management and Budget approval of the relevant information collection, with a separate announcement to follow. Submissions must address reserves, redemption processes, capital, liquidity, risk management and compliance.

For community banks, the immediate task is therefore preparing a regulated issuance business, not treating the invitation as permission to launch.
