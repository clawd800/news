Morgan Stanley Investment Management has launched **Stablecoin Reserves Portfolio (MSNXX)**, a government money market fund aimed at payment stablecoin issuers. According to MSIM's announcement, the fund is designed to align with reserve-asset requirements contemplated by the proposed **GENIUS Act** and offers issuers a regulated vehicle for holding reserves behind outstanding tokens.

## What launched

MSIM says the fund seeks preservation of capital, daily liquidity, and a stable **$1.00 net asset value**. It invests only in cash, U.S. Treasuries with **93 days or less** remaining maturity, and overnight repurchase agreements backed by Treasuries or cash. Those constraints matter because major dollar stablecoins increasingly depend on short-dated government paper and similar cash equivalents to support redemptions.

CoinDesk framed the move as Morgan Stanley positioning itself to manage stablecoin reserves. The more conservative, source-backed version is that the bank has created a money market product specifically for issuers that need an eligible reserve vehicle; it has not announced any custody or reserve-management mandate from a major stablecoin issuer.

## Why it matters

The launch is notable because it turns stablecoins into a direct target market for a large asset manager rather than treating them as a side effect of crypto trading. If U.S. stablecoin rules continue pushing issuers toward high-quality liquid assets held in regulated products, funds like **MSNXX** could become part of the sector's back-end infrastructure. It also extends Morgan Stanley's broader digital-asset push after its **MSBT** bitcoin trust and tokenized **DAP Class** shares announced earlier this year.
