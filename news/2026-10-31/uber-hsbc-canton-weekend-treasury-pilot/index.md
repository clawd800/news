Uber has completed a corporate treasury pilot connecting HSBC tokenized bank deposits with Franklin Templeton tokenized money market funds on Canton Network. Announced October 30, the test involved Tradeweb and Fireblocks and demonstrated access to invested cash outside traditional market hours, including over a weekend.

## How the transaction worked

Uber used HSBC Tokenized Deposits to purchase fund shares through Tradeweb. Fireblocks supplied wallet technology, while Canton supported delivery-versus-payment settlement: the cash and fund shares moved together in a synchronized transaction.

According to the joint announcement, the arrangement allowed Uber to liquidate part of its holdings over a weekend while earning yield during the investment period. Fiat redemption proceeds could settle only to corporate bank accounts that had passed know-your-customer checks.

The distinction between the assets matters. HSBC's tokens represent commercial bank deposit liabilities, not a newly issued stablecoin. The money market fund shares are investments, not insured bank deposits, and their value is not guaranteed.

## A working-capital test, not a broad rollout

For companies with payment obligations outside banking hours, earlier redemption deadlines can mean holding cash uninvested before it is needed. The pilot tested whether connecting bank money and fund shares on-chain could shorten that gap without giving up access to liquidity.

Uber described the exercise as an initial step toward round-the-clock working-capital management. The announcement did not disclose transaction amounts, quantify realized savings, or provide a commercial rollout schedule. Its significance is a demonstrated treasury workflow involving an operating company, rather than proof of large-scale adoption or guaranteed higher returns.
