Salesforce has signed a definitive agreement to acquire **Rune Technologies**, adding AI software for frontline military logistics to its Missionforce defense business. The company announced the agreement on October 29; it should not be read as confirmation that the transaction has closed.

Resilience Media reported on October 30 that the purchase price is **$375 million in cash and shares**, citing two people familiar with the matter. TechCrunch subsequently covered that report. The figure remains a reported deal value, rather than a price disclosed in Salesforce's announcement.

## Connecting field logistics to enterprise systems

Rune builds software to forecast supply needs, optimize resources and support logistics decisions when communications are unreliable. Salesforce says its technology is already deployed with the U.S. Army and U.S. Marine Corps.

The planned integration extends Missionforce beyond headquarters systems into frontline operations. Salesforce describes connecting personnel, equipment and supply records with real-time field data, helping coordinate maintenance, movement and sustainment. It says records can reconcile automatically when connectivity returns.

That connectivity constraint is the concrete technical distinction: logistics software must remain useful when a unit cannot maintain a dependable connection to central enterprise systems. Salesforce positions Rune's agent-powered capabilities as a way to bridge that gap.

## What is confirmed

The verified development is an acquisition agreement and a stated integration direction. The announcement does not establish independently measured improvements in readiness or logistics performance. Those outcomes remain company expectations, while the reported price requires separate attribution.
