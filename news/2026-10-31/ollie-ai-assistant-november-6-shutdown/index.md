Ollie, a privacy-focused AI personal assistant, will shut down on **November 6**, co-founder and CEO Bill Lennon announced on October 30. The closure gives customers roughly a week to prepare for losing access to a service built around household organization.

Lennon attributed the decision to competition and fundraising difficulties. In his announcement, he said the arrival of Muse, Instinct and other assistants made it harder for Ollie to differentiate itself, grow and raise the capital it needed. “We got close, but ultimately couldn't keep up,” he wrote.

## A family-oriented assistant

According to TechCrunch, Ollie offered a subscription service that connected to users' email and calendars and helped with tasks including appointment bookings and meal planning. Privacy was a central part of its positioning, alongside its focus on helping families stay organized.

The shutdown follows a September report on the company's **$7.5 million seed round**, led by Khosla Ventures. That funding history makes the closure notable, but does not establish how much cash remained or precisely what triggered the decision.

## Export and refund instructions to follow

TechCrunch reports that customers will receive instructions for exporting data and obtaining refunds in the coming days. The report does not specify the export process or refund amounts.

For users, the immediate issue is continuity: an assistant connected to everyday scheduling can become part of a household's routine. Ollie's announced end date and forthcoming customer instructions are the concrete next steps; the founder's explanation is an account of this company's difficulties, not evidence that privacy-focused assistants cannot succeed.
