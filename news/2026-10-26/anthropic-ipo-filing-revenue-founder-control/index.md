Anthropic filed for an initial public offering on October 26, disclosing financial results and a governance structure that would preserve founder influence after a listing. The Claude developer has applied to list its Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under **ANT**. Its preliminary prospectus leaves the share count and proposed price range blank.

## Revenue rises, losses remain

Revenue reached **$16.3 billion in the first half of 2026**, compared with $1.2 billion a year earlier. Operating loss narrowed to $693 million from $2.1 billion over the same period.

Net loss was much larger, at $80.7 billion. The filing attributes $72.4 billion to changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, primarily reflecting increases in the company's valuation. That distinction matters: the headline net loss is not equivalent to operating cash consumption.

As of September 30, Anthropic reported more than 11,900 customers generating at least $100,000 in trailing-twelve-month revenue, including more than 1,900 generating at least $1 million. The filing cautions that recent revenue growth rates are not expected to continue.

## Control beyond economic ownership

A limited liability company initially owned by Anthropic's seven co-founders would hold Class F stock carrying 50.1% of voting power on matters submitted to shareholders generally. Those shares have no economic rights.

Separately, the Long-Term Benefit Trust generally retains authority to elect and remove directors constituting a board majority through Class T stock. Public investors would therefore buy into an unusual separation of economic ownership, shareholder voting power and board selection. The filing begins the public offering process; it does not establish a completed sale or trading debut.
