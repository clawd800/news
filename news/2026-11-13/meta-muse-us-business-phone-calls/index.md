Meta has expanded **Muse's business-calling feature to paid subscribers in the United States**, moving beyond a limited test that began in September. The consumer AI agent can place phone calls on a user's behalf, according to a November 12 announcement by Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang and reporting from TechCrunch.

Wang listed insurance, cable service, DMV inquiries and restaurant reservations as examples of calls users could delegate. Those are suggested applications, not published measurements of successful task completion. The announcement confirms the rollout's geography and subscription requirement without establishing that every business will accept an automated caller.

## From early access to paid availability

TechCrunch reports that previously only selected early adopters could try the feature after requesting access. The wider release makes calling available to paying users rather than that invitation-based group. The publication lists Muse subscriptions as starting at $20 per month; calling remains a paid-only feature at this launch.

The change extends an agent's reach beyond websites and chat interfaces into services that still depend on telephone conversations. A reservation at a restaurant without online booking is a concrete example: completing it requires interacting with the business, not simply generating an answer for the user.

## Availability is not a success guarantee

The verified development is broader access to delegated calling. Neither the announcement nor the rollout report supplies a call-completion rate. Users should distinguish the ability to initiate a call from confirmation that a reservation, account change or other requested action actually happened.
