Circle said it has received a limited purpose trust charter from the New York Department of Financial Services for Circle Internet Trust Company LLC, doing business as Circle New York Trust.

The charter gives Circle a New York state trust-company authorization tied to its stablecoin infrastructure. Circle described the approval as part of a longer regulatory relationship with NYDFS, which issued the company its BitLicense in 2015. NYDFS says virtual currency firms can operate in New York through either a BitLicense or a charter under New York Banking Law, including as a limited purpose trust company, when approved for virtual currency business.

The move follows Circle's July approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to establish a national trust bank. The two approvals are not identical. The OCC approval creates a federally supervised trust-bank structure, while the New York charter adds a state-level trust company path for Circle New York Trust.

For USDC, the practical story is regulatory positioning rather than a new consumer product. Circle is continuing to move reserve, custody, and issuance infrastructure closer to bank-style oversight as stablecoin legislation and institutional use of tokenized dollars expand.

The conservative read is that Circle is building redundant regulated rails: federal trust-bank supervision on one side, and New York's established digital-asset framework on the other. That could matter if stablecoin issuers are increasingly judged by the strength and location of their supervisory relationships, not only by market share.
