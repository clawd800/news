Flow Engineering announced a **$50 million Series B at a $750 million valuation** on September 30, financing an expansion of its AI-agent platform for hardware development. The round was co-led by Antonio Gracias of Valor Equity Partners and Gavin Baker of Atreides Management, with Sequoia Capital participating. Roelof Botha also invested personally and joined the board as an independent director.

The company targets a coordination problem that differs from generating software code: a hardware design change can affect mechanical, electrical, and software systems simultaneously. Teams must keep requirements, simulations, and test results aligned as those changes propagate.

Flow describes its platform as a shared system of record connecting requirements, CAD, simulation, code, and testing. Its agents analyze engineering changes, identify downstream effects, and check requirements and test coverage across existing tools. TechCrunch separately reported the financing and described the product as bringing AI agents to hardware design.

According to Flow, customers include Rivian, Anduril, Joby Aviation, and Stoke Space. The company says adoption at Rivian grew from 40 to 1,500 users in seven months; that usage figure is company-reported, not an independently audited performance measure.

The funding will support review, branching, and evaluation capabilities, engineering hiring, and controls for sensitive engineering data. Flow also plans to pursue FedRAMP authorization, rather than claiming it already holds that approval.

The concrete development is funding for agent-assisted engineering coordination. Flow's ambition to compress hardware iteration from months to days remains a stated goal, not a broadly demonstrated outcome established by this announcement.
