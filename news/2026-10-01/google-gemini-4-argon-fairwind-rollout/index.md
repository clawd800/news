Google announced **Gemini 4 Argon** on September 30, beginning a restricted rollout to trusted cybersecurity defenders through its Fairwind Program. The company says broader access will follow, starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers; the announcement does not give a general-release date.

## Longer tasks, larger outputs

A concrete change is an **output limit of one million tokens**, up from the previous 64,000-token limit cited by Google. This is an output allowance, not a claim about the model’s input context window. Google positions the additional capacity as support for extended reasoning and multi-step professional work.

Google says its engineers are already using Argon for debugging, algorithm design and large codebase migrations. It describes agents helping migrate C and C++ software to Rust, while emphasizing that critical rewrites undergo automated and manual auditing, emulation testing and review before production deployment.

## Restricted security access

For cybersecurity, Google says Argon can find, validate and patch software vulnerabilities. Trusted defenders and internal teams will receive access without cyber guardrails, while Google continues developing safeguards ahead of broader distribution. TechCrunch independently reported the limited Fairwind rollout and the model’s defensive-security focus.

Google lists introductory API pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. A footnote says those rates will rise to $4 and $20 after the introductory period.

The immediate significance is a new model entering controlled operational use, rather than a broadly available developer upgrade. Its claimed engineering and security gains remain company-reported results; wider access will give outside teams more opportunity to evaluate them on their own workloads.
