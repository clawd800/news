Robinhood Chain is evaluating a transaction-ordering system that would let traders pay for priority, CoinDesk reported on October 9, citing a person familiar with the matter. **The report describes an evaluation, not a confirmed rollout.** Robinhood declined to comment, and Offchain Labs had not responded to the publication's request for comment.

The technology under consideration is Arbitrum's **Priority Gas Auctions**, rather than its original Timeboost system. Arbitrum publicly announced Priority Gas Auctions and a separate Fast Feed product as live on September 24. That announcement confirms the infrastructure's availability, but does not establish that Robinhood has adopted it.

## From arrival order to paid priority

According to CoinDesk, Robinhood Chain currently orders transactions on a first-come, first-served basis and did not adopt the original Timeboost application. The newer auction model allows traders to offer higher fees for individual transactions to be processed ahead of others.

For a network focused on tokenized financial assets, that would change how participants compete for time-sensitive trading opportunities. It could make transaction fees, alongside arrival timing, a factor in execution order. The report does not establish Robinhood-specific pricing, implementation details or a deployment date.

Paid sequencing also differs from payment for order flow in traditional brokerage: it concerns a transaction's position in a blockchain queue, rather than a market maker paying a broker to route customer orders.

The concrete development is therefore a reported review of market-infrastructure design. Whether Robinhood proceeds, and what protections or access terms accompany any change, remains unconfirmed.
